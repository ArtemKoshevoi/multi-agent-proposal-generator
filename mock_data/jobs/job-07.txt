What we're building
A consumer Android product where the UI is the product. Think: every tap should feel like it pushed a button on a high-end console — satisfying click, weighty animation, perfect spring physics, the kind of micro-interactions that keep people coming back. We're chasing that "one more time" feeling — the small dopamine hits that great mobile games and category-leading consumer apps nail and most apps completely miss.

If you've ever rebuilt a screen three times because the easing curve was off by 50ms, you're our person.
Who we're looking for
You are a senior Android engineer (5+ years), fluent in Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, who treats UI as a craft, not a checkbox. You ship pixel-perfect layouts. You understand frame budgets, recompositions, and why a 60fps animation that drops one frame feels worse than one that's slightly slower but rock-steady. You write Compose like a native speaker — Modifier, LaunchedEffect, derivedStateOf, Animatable, graphicsLayer, custom layouts — and reach for Canvas or shaders without flinching when the design calls for it.

Strongly preferred: background in gaming, gambling, iGaming, or any product where retention and feel are everything. You know what a "juicy" interaction looks like — particle bursts, haptics paired with motion, sound + visual coupling, satisfying win states, anticipation/payoff loops. You've shipped something where you can point at a specific screen and say "I made that feel good, and here's how."
What you'll do
You'll own the Android client end-to-end alongside our small core team. That means:

Translating Figma into Compose so faithfully the designer can't tell which screenshots are which.
Building custom components when Material 3 isn't enough — gesture-driven sheets, complex shared-element transitions, particle effects, parallax, custom Canvas drawing.
Tuning every animation's curve, duration, and stagger until it feels right. Not "looks right" — feels right.
Hitting 60fps (and 120 where supported) on mid-range devices, not just flagships.
Pairing with the designer on motion specs and pushing back when something looks great in After Effects but won't survive a real device.
